# POD Strategy Reference — All Options
Sources: Alek @alekSheffy (Etsy/organic, Video 1) | We Scale / Meg + Eric (DTC/Shopify, Video 2)
Last updated: 2026-06-01

A menu of strategies at each major decision point. Pick what fits your situation.

---

## 1. Platform Choice

**Option A — Alek (Etsy/organic):** Sell on Etsy as a marketplace, relying entirely on organic search traffic from Etsy's algorithm. No paid ads are required to start, and no storefront infrastructure is needed beyond an Etsy account. | **Pros:** Zero upfront ad spend; Etsy brings built-in buyers; lower barrier to entry. | **Cons:** Success depends on listing volume and SEO; you share the marketplace with competitors; algorithm changes can affect visibility. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — We Scale (DTC/Shopify + Facebook Ads):** Build a branded Shopify store and drive traffic via Facebook Dynamic Product Ads (catalog ads). The store is independent of any marketplace. | **Pros:** Higher price points possible (no side-by-side competitor comparison); own your customer relationship; faster design validation via ad metrics. | **Cons:** Requires monthly ad budget ($500+ to test meaningfully); Shopify subscription; steeper setup curve. | *Requires own Shopify store + ad budget.*

## 2. Blank / Garment Selection

**Option A — Alek (Comfort Colors 1717 / Gildan 5000):** Start with blanks at the top of the Printify best-seller list, verified by weekly/monthly/yearly print volume. At recording, the Comfort Colors 1717 garment-dyed tee ranked #2 yearly. Gildan 5000 is the entry-level workhorse used in workflow examples. | **Pros:** Proven demand; large color catalog; well-known quality signals for buyers. | **Cons:** Rankings shift; verify the current Printify best-seller list before committing.

**Option B — We Scale (Gildan 64000):** Choose the Gildan 64000 (softer ringspun cotton) as the primary blank. We Scale explicitly reject the Gildan 5000 as the "itchy free t-shirt you got at a high school football game" and argue a softer blank supports higher price points. | **Pros:** Better perceived quality; enables $29.99+ pricing without resistance. | **Cons:** Higher production cost than the 5000; fewer color options than Comfort Colors.

## 3. Niche & Product Research

**Option A — Alek (sub-niche slogan validation):** Search a broad Etsy category (e.g., "Christmas shirts"), identify specific sayings or slogans that appear across multiple listings from different shops, then search that exact phrase to find a lower-competition pocket with proven demand. Confirm safety by verifying that multiple shops each with 3,000+ reviews all sell the same saying styled differently. | **Pros:** Finds real demand with lower competition; built-in copyright safety signal; directly surfaces the keywords to use. | **Cons:** Requires manual Etsy browsing and pattern recognition; heuristic safety check, not a legal guarantee. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — We Scale (60-minute competitor scraping + AI pattern analysis):** Spend exactly 60 minutes on Etsy, screenshot 10–15 best-selling designs with demand signals ("X bought in 24h," "Bestseller," "Etsy's pick"). Upload all screenshots to an AI research tool (GPT or Gemini). Ask it to identify 3 visual/conceptual patterns across the designs. Stop researching, start creating. | **Pros:** Time-boxed to prevent analysis paralysis; cross-niche opportunities surface from the AI demographic breakdown. | **Cons:** Requires AI tool access; the 60-minute cap may miss nuances for saturated niches.

**Option C — Cross-niche formula (both sources):** Identify what your target niche audience also cares about and combine them: "[Niche] x [complementary interest]" (e.g., golf x beer, golf x gaming, nurse x coffee). Both courses independently recommend this as a design direction multiplier. | **Pros:** Opens new audience segments from a single proven niche; infinite variations. | **Cons:** Not all crossovers resonate — test before scaling.

## 4. Design Approach

**Option A — Alek (design hacking — element extraction):** Extract five types of elements from best-selling listings separately — fonts, color palettes, graphic styles, effects, and slogans — then reassemble them into original designs. Never copy a single listing; sample from many sources. Use a structured "design hacking board" in Kittl (or Canva) with separate artboards for each element type. | **Pros:** Produces designs proven to match Etsy's "handmade, vintage, minimalist" aesthetic; templates scale to 50–100 designs per niche. | **Cons:** Requires Kittl or Canva and optional Creative Fabrica subscription; initial setup of the hacking board takes time. | *Etsy marketplace only (aesthetic guidance specific to Etsy buyer expectations).*

**Option B — We Scale (AI-generated designs + volume):** Use AI image generation (ChatGPT and Gemini in parallel) to produce 100 designs, aiming for 5–10% to become winners. Run each prompt in both tools simultaneously since they produce different styles. Follow up prompts to fix text placement, add negative space, and adjust layout. | **Pros:** Fast volume production; no manual design skills required; AI style variety helps find winners. | **Cons:** Requires prompt iteration and cleanup; AI-generated designs need background removal and upscaling; some outputs will look like "AI slop."

**Option C — Universal design principles (both sources):** Regardless of tool, both courses agree on core principles: simple is better, maximum 1–3 colors, negative space essential (the shirt fabric is part of the design), readable from 10 feet away, one clear message, no color gradients, no block-of-ink designs. | **Pros:** Applies on any platform with any tool. | **Cons:** Principles alone do not produce volume — you still need a workflow.

## 5. Pricing Model

**Option A — Alek (target $7 net profit / ~30% net margin after Etsy fees):** Set prices so that after all Etsy fees, production cost, and shipping, you keep at least $7 per sale. Use the Listing View calculator to verify per-product before committing. Example: Comfort Colors 1717 at $17.99 + $5.99 shipping = ~27% net margin, $6.50/sale — within acceptable range. | **Pros:** Simple floor to check; accounts for real Etsy fee structure. | **Cons:** Dollar threshold needs adjusting as fees change; requires per-product verification. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — Alek / our workflow (cost × 2.5 formula, 60% gross margin):** Set list price at Printify cost × 5, run a permanent 50% off sale, so the effective sell price is cost × 2.5. This targets approximately 60% gross margin before Etsy fees. | **Pros:** Simple multiplier; permanent sale badge improves Etsy search placement. | **Cons:** Does not automatically account for Etsy fees — verify net margin separately. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option C — We Scale (flat price point $29.99, tested range $14.99–$34.99):** Price the Gildan 64000 at $29.99 for S–XL, add $1 per extended size. This yields approximately $16–20 gross profit per shirt before ads (~60% gross), derived from price-point testing rather than a formula. | **Pros:** DTC removes side-by-side price comparison with competitors; premium pricing sustainable on a branded store. | **Cons:** May not work on Etsy where buyers compare prices directly across listings. | *Requires own Shopify store + ad budget.*

## 6. Variant Selection

**Option A — Alek (max 12 non-required color variants, top 6 from Printify data + 6 from competitor/preference):** Use Printify's Insights Dashboard best-selling variants table to identify the top 6 colors for your specific blank by actual sales volume, then add 6 more from competitor reference or personal preference. Cap at 12 colors to prevent buyer fatigue. | **Pros:** Data-driven color selection; cap is evidence-based. | **Cons:** Printify dashboard UI may change; requires checking per blank/model.

**Option B — We Scale (6–8 colors — black, white, navy + 3–4 niche-relevant):** Always include black, white, and navy as the core three (historically highest sellers). Add 3–4 more that visually work with the design or match the niche. Tested both 14 colors (conversion dropped to 1.5%) and 3 colors — 6–8 is the confirmed optimal range. Target conversion rate: 3–4%. | **Pros:** Specific testing data behind the range; niche-matching colors add personality. | **Cons:** 6–8 is a smaller set than Alek's 12 — may limit SKU coverage.

## 7. Mockup Strategy

**Option A — Alek (research-driven mockup selection from Etsy outlier listings):** Before choosing any mockup, search Etsy for your product, use Listing View's outlier score to identify listings performing above average for their shop, note which mockup styles those listings use, then buy those exact mockups from Etsy sellers. Choose based on conversion data, not personal preference. | **Pros:** Mockups proven to convert on the actual platform; data-driven selection. | **Cons:** Requires Listing View subscription; takes time upfront. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — Alek (mockup automation — Figma or Photoshop):** Automate mockup generation using either Figma's component/variant system (free) or a Photoshop script that generates all design × mockup combinations in one batch run. Start with Figma, graduate to Photoshop once making sales. | **Pros:** Scales to hundreds of mockups without manual work; Figma is free. | **Cons:** Photoshop requires $20/month Adobe subscription and a script file from the course materials.

**Option C — We Scale (flatlay only, AI-generated, t-shirt filling the frame):** Generate flatlay mockups using an AI mockup tool (Gemini with the WeScale Mockup Gem). Target 1,080×1,080 square format optimized for Facebook feed. T-shirt sleeves should nearly touch the corners of the image; minimal on-brand props; natural shadows. Generate 10 variations per design and let ad performance identify the winner. | **Pros:** Fast and low-cost; specifically optimized for Facebook feed scroll context. | **Cons:** Flatlay-only approach may underperform on Etsy where lifestyle/model shots are common. | *Requires own Shopify store + ad budget.*

## 8. Listing Optimization (Etsy-specific)

**Option A — Title: human-readable first 30 characters + keyword tail (Alek):** First ~30 characters of the title must be a plain-English description of the product (what shows in search result truncation). Remaining characters are keyword phrases pulled from top-selling competitors via Listing View and shuffled to ensure each listing in a batch has a unique title. | **Pros:** Matches Etsy's stated preference for human-friendly titles; addresses search truncation. | **Cons:** May conflict with placing the highest-volume keyword first. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — Tags: prioritize Listing View recommendation score (Alek):** Select the 2–3 tags with the highest recommendation score (combines low competition + high search volume + conversion signal) and apply to all listings in a batch. Fill remaining slots by rotating tags from multiple top-selling competitor listings. Do not avoid high-competition tags — Etsy promotes new listings even in heavily competed searches. | **Pros:** Recommendation score is more informative than raw volume alone. | **Cons:** Requires Listing View subscription. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option C — Description: keywords at top, upsell links at bottom, send buyers back to photos (Alek):** Description structure — keywords section first (improves indexing), production/shipping times, how to order, specs, then upsell links to related listings. End with a line directing buyers back to the photos section at the top of the page, preventing them from scrolling to Etsy's competing listings panel at the bottom. | **Pros:** Practical page-retention tactic; upsell links increase average order value. | **Cons:** Etsy's algorithm weighting of description keywords may change. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

## 9. Traffic & Marketing

**Option A — Alek (organic Etsy traffic, volume-first):** Do not run any paid ads until a listing already makes organic sales. The only lever is listing volume plus keyword optimization. Benchmark: ~1,000 quality listings = $1,000–$2,000/month profit. Etsy's algorithm tests every new listing; positive interactions (favorites, add-to-cart, purchases) signal it to promote the listing further. | **Pros:** Zero ad spend required; organic traffic compounds over time; each listing continues to sell passively. | **Cons:** Slow to build; requires sustained daily publishing volume; seasonal timing must account for 2–3 week indexing lag. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — Alek (Etsy ads on organic winners only — CPC method):** Only activate Etsy ads on listings already making organic sales. Use the CPC formula to determine profitability: (conversion rate % × profit per sale) ÷ 100 = max CPC budget. If actual CPC exceeds this at 100 clicks, turn the ad off. Do not rely on ROAS alone — it obscures per-unit economics. | **Pros:** Math-based kill switch; only spends on proven listings. | **Cons:** Requires 50–100 clicks of data before a firm decision; highly competitive niches may make Etsy ads unrunnable. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option C — We Scale (Facebook Dynamic Product Ads / catalog ads):** Drive all traffic via Facebook catalog ads that automatically show all products in the Shopify store. Target under $1.00 cost-per-click as the green-light signal. Run ads Thursday–Sunday, design Monday–Wednesday, analyze Monday. The catalog ad auto-updates as new products are added — no manual campaign changes needed for new designs. | **Pros:** Validates designs quickly via ad metrics; scalable spend once winning designs are identified. | **Cons:** Requires ongoing ad budget; takes time for Facebook to optimize; beginners often aren't profitable immediately. | *Requires own Shopify store + ad budget.*

**Option D — Alek (Etsy email automations — set up once):** Configure all three Etsy-native email automations before the first sale: abandoned cart (~45–50% off), favorited item (~55% off), thank you/repeat purchase (~55–60% off). Set coupon percentages to match or slightly exceed the daily sale discount to prevent stacking. These run permanently with no ongoing work. | **Pros:** Free; compounds with catalog size; an example shop generated $8,000 extra profit from favorite-item automation alone. | **Cons:** Must be set up before the first interaction to capture all signals from day one. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

## 10. Mockup Strategy

**Option A — Alek (data-driven mockup research via eRank Listing View):** Don't pick mockups based on personal taste. Search Etsy for your product + niche, open eRank Listing View to see outlier scores (how many times better a listing sells vs. other listings in the same shop). Note which mockup styles the high-outlier listings use. Search Etsy for that exact mockup pack — the top result is usually the same one you just saw performing well. Buy it ($3–5). | **Pros:** Mockup proven to convert on the actual platform before you spend a dollar on it; cheap. | **Cons:** Requires eRank account; mock trends evolve — re-research each niche. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — We Scale (AI-generated flatlay, 10 variations per design):** Generate top-down flatlay mockups via AI (Gemini prompts): clean neutral background, shirt taking up majority of the 1080×1080 frame with sleeves nearly touching corners, 2–3 niche-relevant props below the print zone, realistic shadows. Generate 10 variations per design and test which performs best in Facebook ads. | **Pros:** Fast at scale; no mockup purchase required. | **Cons:** Flatlay-only may underperform on Etsy where lifestyle/model shots are common. | *Built for Facebook ads → Shopify; not recommended as the Etsy default.*

**What to avoid (both sources):** Do NOT use Placeit. Alek: "You'll never find best-selling listings on Etsy using a Placeit mockup." Placeit is immediately identifiable as non-handmade. Buy from Etsy sellers instead — already proven, cheap, and authentic-looking.

**What makes a good mockup (universal):**
- Design clearly readable — not too small, nothing overlapping the print area
- Shirt looks like it's actually on a surface (realistic shadows, natural wrinkles — not floating)
- Bright, not cluttered — product + person or 1–2 props max
- Aesthetic of the mockup matches the aesthetic of the design

**Etsy listing photo slots (Alek):** Use 5–10 photos. Slot 1 = best-performing mockup (the scroll-stopper). Slots 2–4 = additional styles (flat lay, lifestyle, close-up, alternate colorway). Slot 7 = color chart (all available colors with names). Slot 8 = sizing guide with measurements. Keep slots 7 and 8 in the same position across every listing — makes bulk updating easier. Add video if possible: listings with video convert ~2× better and only ~28% of sellers use them.

## 10. Scaling Your Catalog

**Option A — Alek (5-variation multiplier from any winning listing):** When any listing makes a sale, immediately create 5 more variations of that same design using different mockups and colorways as the main listing image, each published as a separate listing. This gives 6 total listings targeting the same validated concept. Also translate the winning design template across niches: swap the niche-specific element to open a different audience. | **Pros:** Multiplies proven winners with minimal effort; Etsy's algorithm actively rewards shops that convert. | **Cons:** Requires discipline to act on every sale immediately rather than continuing to create new designs. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — We Scale (50/50 rule — iterate winners + fresh research in parallel):** Half of all new designs should be concept-level iterations of winning designs (not minor tweaks — different jokes, different emotional angles, different graphic metaphors). The other half should come from new research. Never make minor execution tweaks (changing a font, adjusting a color) — these address the same micro-audience. Iterate at the concept level to open new audience segments. Target: 25 new designs per week consistently. | **Pros:** Balances exploitation of proven concepts with exploration of new opportunities; weekly rhythm compounds over time. | **Cons:** Requires analyzing ad performance data to identify which designs are winning. | *Requires own Shopify store + ad budget.*

**Option C — Volume targets (both sources):** Both courses agree that design volume is the master input. Alek: 20 listings/day target; 1,000 listings = $1,000–$2,000/month profit benchmark; top shops have 5,000+ listings after ~2 years. We Scale: top 1% of POD sellers make 7 designs/day; community data shows 52%+ of $10K+ sellers have 250–500 live designs. | **Pros:** Clear milestones to aim for regardless of platform. | **Cons:** Volume without quality and research does not produce proportional results.

## 11. Diagnosing Poor Performance

**Option A — Alek (four-stage diagnostic funnel):** Work top-down through four stages. Stage 1 — No views: SEO is broken; fix title, tags, and description. Stage 2 — Views but no clicks: image problem; fix main mockup, thumbnail crop, or design clarity. Stage 3 — Clicks but no interactions (no favorites, no add-to-cart): design quality is poor, info cards are missing, or price is too high. Stage 4 — Interactions but no purchases: fix trust signals (reviews, video, shop appearance) and check which color/size variants you're offering. | **Pros:** Specific fix for each stage; prevents misdiagnosis. | **Cons:** Requires enough traffic volume to distinguish between stages reliably. | *Etsy marketplace only.*

**Option B — We Scale (three-metric funnel — traffic quality, conversion rate, AOV — in order):** Fix one funnel variable at a time, always in this sequence: (1) cost-per-click under $1.00, (2) conversion rate above 2.5% (sub-metrics: add-to-cart 6–8%, reach checkout 5–6%), (3) AOV above $45 before scaling spend. Do not optimize AOV before conversion is proven. Do not optimize the store before traffic quality is proven. | **Pros:** Order of operations prevents wasted effort; clear numerical benchmarks at each stage. | **Cons:** Metrics assume Facebook ads traffic; Etsy equivalents (CTR, views-to-visits ratio) would need to be substituted. | *Requires own Shopify store + ad budget.*

**Option C — P&L discipline (We Scale):** Run a profit and loss statement at minimum weekly, ideally daily. Track cost of goods as a percentage of revenue (target 40–42%). Audit all software subscriptions monthly as a percentage of revenue (target under 3%). Overhead creep is a silent killer — one brand unknowingly reached $35,000/month in software fees. "Profit is a choice." | **Pros:** Applies to any business model; catches margin erosion before it compounds. | **Cons:** Requires a consistent accounting habit that most beginners skip early.
