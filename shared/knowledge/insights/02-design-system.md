## The Sub-niche Method (core concept)
**Evergreen** — buyer psychology and search behavior principle

This is explicitly flagged as "the real sauce" and the section "that's going to make you more sales than the rest of the course combined."

### The problem with broad niches
Searching "Christmas shirts" returns ~3 million competing listings. Winning there as a new seller is nearly impossible. The sub-niche method drills down inside that broad niche to find lower-competition pockets that are still generating real sales.

### How to identify a sub-niche
1. Do a broad niche search on Etsy (e.g. "Christmas shirts").
2. Scroll through the first page of results. Look for the same **saying or slogan appearing across multiple listings** from different shops.
3. When you spot a repeated saying (e.g. "Merry and Bright"), open a new tab and search for that exact saying + product type (e.g. "merry and bright shirt").
4. The new search will show dramatically less competition. His examples:
   - "Christmas shirts" → ~3 million listings
   - "Merry and bright shirt" → ~170,000 listings
   - "Tis the season shirt" → lower still
   - "Probably just going to jingle" → ~6,000 listings
5. Confirm the sub-niche is safe and viable: look for **multiple large shops (thousands of reviews) all selling the same saying**. His rule: "A shop with 57,000 reviews isn't going to stake their entire business on selling a product that actually has a copyright behind it."

### The safety check for slogans
"The general rule of thumb is that if a lot of large shops are all using the same exact slogan on their T shirt or any product that they're selling, just styled in different ways, it's likely that not one specific shop owns the entire copyright for that design." He specifies: "multiple very successful shops that all have around over **3,000 reviews**" using the same slogan = safe signal.

### Sub-sub-niching
You can go one level deeper. Example: "Christmas" → "gingerbread" → "Oh snap gingerbread shirt" (~4,000 listings, Etsy promoting new listings, big shops selling the same saying).

### The math / batching logic
- Pick one broad niche. Find 10 sub-niches within it.
- Make 5–10 designs per sub-niche = **50–100 designs from one niche idea**.
- "I just turned a category into a specific niche. And then I broke that niche into ten sub niches where I can go create 5 to 10 designs for each one of those sub niches."
- First batch = also the template. Subsequent sub-niches reuse the same design templates, just swapping the saying. He demonstrated turning 8 "Merry and Bright" designs into 5+ "Tis the Season" designs in seconds.

### Why it matters for ranking
Targeting a specific sub-niche saying is "much easier to get our products to rank for than something like a broad Christmas shirt. This gives us an exact saying to use in a lot of our products."

⚠️ He acknowledges: "you have to be extremely careful and it's totally up to you to know and choose how to proceed" — the safety check is a heuristic, not a legal guarantee.

## What to Copy vs What to Avoid
**Evergreen**

### What to copy (extract/sample)
Design hacking = "reverse engineering those winning products into **design elements** that we can use to go create competing products in our own original way."

The five extractable elements from best sellers:
1. **Fonts** — identify via reverse font lookup (WhatTheFont), then find in Kittl or source from Creative Fabrica
2. **Colors** — use the eyedropper tool to extract exact color palettes; save as swatches
3. **Graphics** — source from Creative Fabrica, buy SVG packs on Etsy, or generate via AI
4. **Effects** — distressed textures, text transforms (arch, flag, wave), shadows, outlines
5. **Slogans** — save reusable templates like "eat, sleep, [activity], repeat" or "I'd rather be [activity]"

"You can use the exact same fonts, colors, graphic styles, mockups, and effects that best selling listings use."

### What NOT to do
- Do not copy any single listing entirely
- Do not copy a specific shop's design
- Do not use pre-made Placeit templates — "everyone has access to the exact same templates, which means everyone's selling the same exact thing"
- Do not use Kittl's pre-built text templates — "sometimes it'll lead you astray from that Etsy style... A lot of these are just generally good looking text templates, but that doesn't necessarily mean that they're going to sell well on Etsy"

### The framing rule
"It's absolutely not about stealing anyone's design or idea, it's simply about learning why it worked... when you're sampling individual elements from lots of different best selling listings, you truly do create something that's original."

## Design Hacking Playbook
**Check** (tool-specific: Kittl, Listing View, WhatTheFont, Creative Fabrica)

### Step 1 — Research
- Use Listing View Chrome extension on Etsy
- His minimum criteria for saving a listing: **15 sales per month** (ties to the "15/100 rule": 100 listings × 15 sales/month × ~$7 profit = ~$10k/month)
- Save listings to Listing View watchlist OR screenshot via hotkey (Shift+Cmd+tilde) and paste into Kittl design hacking board
- Pay more attention to newer listings that are selling well ("if a listing is newer and it's selling super well, I'm going to reference that one a little bit more")

### Step 2 — Design Hack (extract elements)
- Work from a free Kittl "Design Hacking Board" template (provided in course downloadable materials)
- Build artboards for: Fonts, Colors, Graphics, Effects, Slogans
- Font extraction: screenshot text → WhatTheFont.com → find or buy font → upload to Kittl
- Color extraction: rectangle + eyedropper tool → save swatch blocks
- Graphic sourcing: 3 options — (a) pre-made SVG/PNG from Creative Fabrica or Etsy, (b) AI-generated via Kittl's Nano Banana Pro model, (c) Kittl's built-in illustrations (simple elements only)
- AI graphics workflow: crop reference design into artboard → add smart board → write prompt ("using the same style of graphic, create a gingerbread man") → generate (30–40s) → bulk remove backgrounds → use in designs
- Slogan extraction: save reusable templates; replace bracketed words to create new designs

### Step 3 — Batch
- Make as many design variations as possible from the extracted elements
- First batch = template for future sub-niches
- "The more time that you spend on the initial batch of products and creating these templates as best as you can, the easier time you're going to have creating every future sub niche"
- Target: 8+ designs per batch ("I only spent about 15 to 20 minutes on this first batch, but it let me create an additional five designs in 20 seconds or so")

## Fonts, Colors & Graphics That Sell
**Evergreen** (principles) / **Check** (specific tools)

### Fonts
- Kittl provides free fonts covering most needs; supplement with Creative Fabrica (~$4/month or $47/year)
- Creative Fabrica: "commercial and full print on demand usage allowed" — critical; not all fonts grant commercial use
- Buying fonts individually can "really add up over time" — subscription preferred
- Two specific fonts mentioned as popular Etsy POD fonts: **Margin** and **Nectarine** (both available on Creative Fabrica)
- File formats: OTF or TTF; upload directly into Kittl font library
- Match font weight to word count: "When you make designs that have less words, you're going to want to use a wider, fatter font. When you use more words per line, you're going to need thinner, skinnier fonts."

### Colors
- "Classic black and white designs... have done extremely well for me in my personal shop. They do tend to work the best sometimes."
- Always produce both a black and a white version of every design
- For white text on apparel: use **#FBB1B** (slightly yellowish white) instead of pure white — "it tricks the actual printers... to lay in two layers of ink... makes all of the prints last a lot longer, and it makes them more vivid over time"

⚠️ The hex code as spoken is "#FBB1B" — this may be a transcription artifact. Verify the exact value from the course downloadable materials.

- Black design → light-colored shirts (white, ivory only)
- White design → all other/dark-colored shirts

### Graphics
- Etsy style to target: "handmade, minimalist, vintage bohemian illustrated vibe" — NOT "cartoonish Mimi style designs that work really well on Amazon and eBay"
- Keywords for Etsy graphic style: handmade, minimalist, vintage, bohemian, illustrated
- AI graphics (Kittl Nano Banana Pro): "by far the best model to be using inside of Kittl... you can write super simple prompts while still getting really high quality graphic results"
- Pre-made SVG packs on Etsy: "16 people bought in the last 24 hours" is a signal of popularity, but high saturation risk; look for shops that "drop new graphics regularly" to be among first listers
- Negative space rule: "instead of having this be white in a part of the graphic, you would want this to be negative space so that if the color of the t shirt actually changes, it'll show through"
- Distressed texture: popular depending on niche — heavy distress works in some niches, less so in Christmas designs; apply via alpha mask in Kittl

## Writing Slogans That Convert
**Evergreen**

### Reusable slogan templates
These are fill-in-the-blank structures that work across niches by swapping one word:
- "Eat, sleep, [activity], repeat"
- "I'd rather be [activity]"
- "I wish I was [doing an activity]"

"You just replace one of the bracketed words with a word from any niche, and it creates a brand new design based on the exact same template and design structure."

### How to find new slogans
The sub-niche method IS the slogan-finding method. Scroll best-seller pages for repeated sayings → search that saying → validate with the 3,000-review multi-shop check. His Christmas examples: "Merry and Bright," "Tis the Season," "It's the Most Wonderful Time," "Come Let Us Adore Him," "Feeling Jolly," "Just a Jolly Goose," "Oh Snap" (gingerbread), "Probably Just Going to Jingle."

### The key principle on style vs. content
"It's not necessarily about the specific words or the exact slogan that you put on the product. It's about the font and the way that you style that saying."

## Trademarks & Copyright
**Evergreen** (legal principles) / ⚠️ Disclaimer: "I am not a lawyer. This entire course is just for entertainment purposes only."

### Copyright
- Automatic — "it happens automatically the moment something is created with no registration required"
- You cannot use: others' photos, artwork, SVG files you didn't create or buy with commercial rights, designs without permission
- Copyright strikes can happen even without official registration
- Your own designs are also automatically protected
- Rule: "Be original. Use design hacking to sample elements, not copy entirely."
- Fonts and graphics: must have **commercial use rights** — Creative Fabrica subscription covers this; verify before using any downloaded asset

### Trademark
- "Identifies a brand — name, logo, slogan, or symbol"
- Cannot use: Disney, Taylor Swift, NFL, Harry Potter, Nike swoosh, "Just Do It", Coca-Cola — "even if you create original designs yourself, because the idea has already been trademarked"
- Check tool: **USPTO database** — filter by "live" trademarks only to avoid expired results
- Course provides a trademark checker link in downloadable materials
- "If something is too close, then definitely don't list it for sale"

### The multi-shop safety heuristic
If multiple large shops (3,000+ reviews each) are all selling the same slogan styled differently, that saying is likely safe — no single shop owns its copyright. This is the same check used in the Sub-niche Method above. "This tactic can bring in sales if done correctly, but you have to be extremely careful."

## SEO on Printify Uploads
**Check** (tool-specific: Printify AI, Pre-list)

### Manual upload method (small batches)
1. Copy the template product in Printify
2. Edit the design — delete old design, upload black version, assign to light colors (white + ivory only), upload white version for all other colors
3. Use Printify's "Improve Listing" AI: tell it what the product is (e.g. "a Christmas shirt that says Merry and Bright") → it generates a title, tags, and description → hit Apply
4. Save all first, then publish in bulk

### Bulk upload via Pre-list (10+ designs)
1. Connect Printify shop + import template listing
2. Drag all designs in; add a second variant set for black-on-light colors
3. SEO step: open 2–3 top-selling listings in the target sub-niche → copy their titles → paste all into a text editor → separate all keywords by commas → paste into Pre-list's keywords field
4. Pre-list randomizes keywords across all listings in the batch so "every single product in our batch is going to have a completely unique title with different SEO from one another... super important when you're trying to rank organically on Etsy search"
5. Tags: he adds these later when adding mockups to the live Etsy listing — not in this upload step
6. Hit Upload → Start Creating Products → then Publish to Etsy

### Export settings from Kittl
- DPI: **300**
- Format: **PNG**
- Remove Background: **yes** — ⚠️ this is not optional. A white background PNG prints a white rectangle around the design on every colored shirt. To verify: open the product in Printify and preview on a dark shirt color — if you see a white box, re-export with background removed.
- Export black and white versions as separate folders
- Artboard aspect ratio must match Printify's print area dimensions (find in Printify canvas editor under print area size)

## Key Takeaways (3–5 bullets)

- **Sub-niche first, always.** Never start with a broad niche. Drill down to a specific saying/slogan that multiple 3,000+ review shops are already selling — that intersection of low competition + proven demand + trademark safety is where new listings actually get found and bought.

- **The design IS the product.** "It's the single largest needle mover in terms of making any money." Etsy buyers respond to a specific style (handmade, minimalist, vintage, illustrated) — not just any design that looks good in general. Matching that style matters more than the specific words on the shirt.

- **Design hack elements, not full designs.** Collect fonts (Kittl + Creative Fabrica), extract color palettes, generate AI graphics in the same style, and save slogan templates — then reassemble into original designs. Sampling from many sources = original output.

- **Batch by sub-niche, then reuse templates.** First batch costs 15–20 minutes and produces ~8 designs. Every subsequent sub-niche reuses those templates (swap the saying) and can produce 5+ new designs in under a minute. Scale = 5 designs × 10 sub-niches per niche × hundreds of niches.

- **One black version + one white version, always.** Export at 300 DPI PNG, black design on white/ivory shirts, white (#FBB1B slightly off-white) on all dark shirts. Pre-list randomizes titles per listing to avoid duplicate SEO signals across your own catalog.
