## How Etsy Search Really Works

**Evergreen** — Core algorithm logic (Etsy has not officially published this; Alek's interpretation):

1. **Initial test exposure** — "Etsy is going to test your product listings... they show almost every single listing in search at least once to potential customers."
2. **Interaction signals promote listings** — "If customers positively interact with your products by favoriting them or adding them to cart, then it's going to get promoted and they're going to keep showing it in search."
3. **Inactivity buries listings** — "If your listing doesn't get sales or it stops getting sales or it doesn't get interactions... Etsy is going to stop promoting it."
4. **Etsy's incentive is aligned with yours** — "Etsy wants you to win, because the more sales you make, the more money Etsy makes over time."
5. **Indexing delay** — "Etsy's algorithm is going to read the title tags and descriptions and even the attributes... figure out what the product is about, and store that information in their search database." No clicks are possible until indexing is complete.
6. **Listing volume matters directly** — Top shops have the most listings. "The top shops on Etsy... also have the most amount of listings." One top t-shirt shop had 5,000+ listings after ~2.25 years.
7. **Keywords + adjacent keywords** — Etsy matches listings to searches it "thinks is similar" to your keywords, including searches you didn't explicitly target. Adjacent keywords expand your surface area.

**Ranking signals mentioned (in order of emphasis):**
- Positive interactions (favorites, add-to-cart, purchases, shares)
- Keyword relevance (title, tags, description, attributes)
- Listing volume in shop
- Proven sales history (especially before running ads)

⚠️ Alek notes: "Etsy hasn't disclosed all of this explicitly in public before" — treat algorithm mechanics as observed behaviour, not official documentation.

## Free Traffic Formula

**Evergreen** — Alek's step-by-step organic approach, baked into the entire course structure:

1. **Research before creating** — "I've already done the research ahead of time so that I know everything I list has the absolute best chance of success." Do not create products first and optimize later.
2. **Find a sub-niche within a broader category** — Drill down past top-level categories to specific addressable audiences.
3. **Use proven reference listings for keywords** — "Use the keywords from proven reference listings... use their titles, use their tags, scramble them on your listings." Use Listing View to identify high search volume, low competition keywords.
4. **Use adjacent keywords** — Include terms where "if the customer did search for it, they could still end up buying your product." Example: a "billing by the hour" t-shirt targeting lawyers, therapists, counselors, accountants all at once. Example: "adventure awaits" shirt also targeting camping, wanderlust, hiking, road trips, nature lovers.
5. **Post a large volume of listings** — "The only cheat code I have is... make a lot of products." No other shortcut exists.
6. **Set and forget** — Organic listings "continue to passively sell over time" once indexed. The majority of Alek's catalog runs on organic alone.

**Summary formula (Alek's exact words):** "Great keywords that are proven to drive traffic on Etsy, and then having a lot of listings posted for sale."

**Three-step version (verbatim):**
> "Find a sub niche within a broader category niche. Using listing view to identify those best sellers, use the keywords from proven reference listings... Number three, make more product listings... I promise if you do these three things and you keep doing them over time, you won't be broke ever again."

## Realistic Timeline

**Evergreen** — Reframe from time to listing count:

- "Instead of thinking in terms of time, think in terms of listings. Time isn't a good metric."
- **Indexing lag** — "I typically don't get any traffic from products that I've listed within the first 2 to 3 weeks." **Check** — "Some sources say 24 to 48 hours" but Alek's personal experience is 2–3 weeks.
- **Seasonal planning rule** — "You're going to want to create your products at least a month ahead." Breakdown: ~2 weeks to index + ~1 week for fulfilment/shipping = list at least 3 weeks before the event date, 4 weeks to be safe.
- **Competitive niches index slower** — "For brand new trends and super small niches... indexing is going to be a lot faster. But when there's hundreds of thousands and maybe even millions of competing [listings], that's where it's going to take you a lot longer."
- **Income benchmark** — "On average, over time, I've made around $1 of profit per month for each listing that I've posted for sale." Over 6 years, ~15,000 products ≈ ~$15,000/month average. **Check** — highly personal, your mileage will vary.
- **Conservative target for new sellers** — "If you post 1,000 listings and they're actually good, and you actually follow the steps in this course, you should be able to make between $1,000 and $2,000 of profit per month." **Check** — stated as a rule of thumb, not a guarantee.
- **Organic requires more listings** — "If you are going with the strictly organic approach, it takes more listings since a lot of the listings that you post might not sell."

## Etsy Ads — ROAS Method

**Check** — Specific thresholds may shift as Etsy's ad auction changes.

- **Only run ads on proven organic sellers** — "I only ever turn on the paid ads after a listing is already making sales." Running ads on listings that don't sell organically wastes money: "Paid ads are like adding fuel to the fire, but if there's no fire... you're just dumping cash on a listing that might not ever sell."
- **Break-even ROAS target** — "On average, you need at least a 2 to 3x return on your ad spend to break even and start making profit from your ads." ⚠️ Alek showed one product example where the Listing View calculator indicated a **3x ROAS was needed just to break even** due to discounts and costs — so the actual break-even varies per product.
- **Kill threshold** — "If a listing is consistently under a 2x ROAS at 50 to 100 clicks, then I'd recommend turning it off."
- **100-click rule** — "Typically, you're going to want to wait until your product has at least 50 to 100 clicks on it before you can really know if that listing is going to be profitable." Use 50 clicks as an early signal; 100 clicks for a firm decision.
- **Example kill scenario** — 83 clicks, $69.42 spend, 1.7x ROAS, $0.83 CPC vs. $0.21 break-even CPC → turn off the ad.
- **Etsy ads take time to optimize** — "You're almost always not going to be profitable from day one." Allow Etsy time to learn before evaluating.

## Etsy Ads — CPC Method

**Evergreen** — Math-based approach Alek considers superior to ROAS alone:

- "Personally, I think that the ROAS method pales in comparison to something like the cost per click method."
- **Formula:** `(Conversion Rate % × Profit Per Sale) ÷ 100 = Max CPC budget`
  - Example: 3% conversion × $10 profit ÷ 100 = $0.30 max CPC
  - "This formula tells you exactly how much you can afford to pay for a click while still breaking even."
- **How to calculate current CPC:** `Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Clicks = Current CPC`
  - Example: $0.50 spend ÷ 5 clicks = $0.10 CPC
- **Decision rule:**
  - Current CPC < Max CPC budget → profitable, keep running (or scale)
  - Current CPC > Max CPC budget → unprofitable, turn off
- **Worked profitable example** — 44 clicks, 3 orders (6% conversion), $15.43 spend, 4.4x ROAS, $0.35 CPC vs. $0.60 budget → profitable, double down.
- **Worked borderline example** — 47 clicks, 3 orders (6% conversion), $10 profit product, $0.49 CPC vs. $0.60 budget → profitable by ~$0.11/click; keep running even though ROAS looks close to 2x.
- "Some listings in some niches just cost more to run ads to, since the competition is higher. And so even if a product is good, it doesn't always mean that it's going to work well with ads."

## Ad Optimization

**Evergreen** — How to diagnose and improve underperforming ads:

- **Monitor per listing, not per campaign** — "You're going to want to monitor individual listing performance instead of the overall ad campaign, because the results are going to be very different between each one of your listings."
- **Lots of clicks but few orders → fix the creative, not the ad**
  - "Either your pricing is wrong... or you have a bad preview image or mockup."
  - Action: "Test with different mockups for that listing."
  - Or: "Your design was bad and they couldn't tell from the first page of search results."
- **Goal: attract buyers, repel browsers** — "You actually want less people to click on your ads, but you do want the people who are actually going to buy it to click on them."
  - Fix: crop the thumbnail image tighter in the Etsy listing manager (Listings tab → open listing → edit images → Transform → square crop → zoom in).
  - "Better thumbnail, fewer wasted clicks, lower ad costs, and better profitability over time."
- **Highly competitive niches may be unrunnable** — "If there's a ton of people trying to run ads, then it drives up the total cost per click... sometimes it's unavoidable and it's just too expensive to run ads on Etsy."
- **Monitor continuously** — "The costs per click change and so you want to continuously monitor to make sure that you're always remaining at least at break even and ideally profitably."
- **100-click rule applies to CPC method too** — "Run them on your best listings, but then let them get to at least 100 clicks."

## Key Takeaways (most actionable for someone just launching)

- **Organic first, always.** Don't run paid ads until a listing already makes organic sales. Running ads on a listing that doesn't convert is "dumping cash." Build volume with SEO-optimised listings before touching ads.
- **Volume is the real lever.** Alek's benchmark: ~1,000 quality listings = ~$1,000–$2,000/month profit. There is no shortcut; the "cheat code" is research + volume + repetition.
- **Plan seasonally at least 4 weeks out.** Indexing alone takes 2–3 weeks. Add 1 week for fulfilment. Miss that window and your seasonal listings arrive after the demand peak.
- **Use adjacent keywords to multiply search surface area.** One well-designed t-shirt can rank for 5–10 related search terms if you think about who else would buy it and why.
- **The CPC formula is your ads kill switch.** `(CV% × Profit) ÷ 100 = Max CPC`. If actual CPC exceeds this number at 100 clicks, turn the ad off. Don't rely on ROAS alone — it hides the per-unit economics.
