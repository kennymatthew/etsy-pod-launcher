## Daily Routine

Alek calls this the **"core loop"** — the same list of tasks repeated every single day.

**Morning block:**
1. Handle customer service first — check Etsy messages, reply to all customers
   - Must reply to 95% of messages within 24 hours (required for Star Seller badge)
   - Do this BEFORE fulfilling orders so you can catch cancellation requests or changes before submitting to production
2. Research — find trends, analyze niches, spot demand
   - Add competitor listings and shops to your competitor list
   - Add designs to the design hacking board (saving future designs to make)
   - Check niche calendar: look at what's coming up 1 month away for seasonal planning
3. Batch create designs (follows the design hacking process covered earlier in course)
4. Upload and optimize listings — publish them for sale

**End of day block:**
5. Handle customer service again (second pass)
6. Fulfill all orders

> "Publishing listings is the last growth task throughout the day."

**Evergreen** — routine is platform-agnostic and focused on volume + consistency.

**Time estimate from live demo:** Research + designing + uploading 7 listings completed in ~45 minutes of real recorded time.

**On mindset when you have no sales yet:**
> "Even if you don't have any sales, pretend you're fulfilling orders every single day. It gets you in the habit of doing it."

**Evergreen**

## Weekly Growth Tasks

These are labeled "weekly or as needed" — they go beyond the daily routine.

### 1. Daily Sale (Marketing Routine)
- Run a 50% off sale every day — set start and end date to the same date so it expires at midnight
- Schedule them out a week or month in advance so you never forget and don't have to set them manually each night
- Path: Etsy → Marketing → Sales & Discounts → "Run a Sale"
- Reason: Almost all high-ranking Etsy listings run sales; it creates urgency (countdown timers appear to buyers)

> "There's no way to automate it. So you just kind of got to manually set up your sales."

**Evergreen** (tactic works as long as Etsy shows countdown timers to buyers)

### 2. Email Automations (set up once, then automatic)
Set up all three Etsy email automations:
- **Abandoned cart offer:** ~45–50% off
- **Favorite item offer:** ~55% off
- **Thank you offer:** ~55–60% off

Key trick: Set the coupon percentage to be the same as (or slightly different from) your daily sale so customers cannot stack discounts — Etsy will tell them they're already getting the sale discount.

**Custom promo code names he uses:**
- Abandoned cart: `left on red`
- Favorite item: `great choice`
- Thank you: `see you soon`

> "This is pretty underutilized in my opinion."

Calculation example given: A shop with 50,000 total listing favorites converting just 2% through automation = 1,000 extra sales × $8 profit = $8,000 extra profit for no ongoing work.

**Evergreen** — as long as Etsy supports these automations.

### 3. Seasonal/Holiday Calendar Planning
- Use an upcoming calendar to identify events 1 month out and plan designs accordingly
- Example: In December, plan for Lunar New Year, MLK Jr., Valentine's Day

**Evergreen**

### 4. Customer Service Systems (set up once)
- **Replacement listing:** Create a listing priced at exact print cost + Etsy fee (no profit markup). Used when a customer orders wrong size/color. They purchase the replacement listing; you manually fulfill with correct variant.
  - Formula: `print cost + shipping cost + $0.20 Etsy listing fee = replacement listing price`
  - Example given: $10.72 + $3.99 + $0.20 = $14.91
  - Result: You keep all profit; customer is still happy
  - > "Nine out of ten times the customer is going to take the replacement listing."
- **Canned responses (Quick Replies):** Pre-write replies for all common scenarios — sizing questions, cancellation requests, custom product photos, lost in mail, incorrect address, returns and exchanges
  - He provides a downloadable document of all his quick replies
  - Categories match all typical support scenarios

**Evergreen**

### 5. International Selling Prep (Monthly)
- For EU/UK sales: Go to Printify → Manage Stores → Settings → enable the EU compliance checkbox
- Printify pre-fills their own business information for EU compliance so you don't have to supply it yourself
- ⚠️ He notes "international laws and rules change all the time" — this specific step may need re-verification

**Check** — regulations change; verify current EU/UK requirements.

## Not Getting Sales? Diagnosis Framework

Alek calls this the **"diagnostic funnel."** It works top-down — diagnose at each stage before moving to the next.

```
VIEWS → VISITS → INTERACTIONS → CONVERSIONS
```

### Stage 1: No Views
**Problem:** Your listings are not showing up in search results at all.
**Cause:** You did not follow the SEO tactics covered in the course.
**Fix:** Go back and rewatch the SEO and listing optimization tutorials.

> "If you're not getting any views, it's because you didn't follow any of the SEO tactics that I've shown you."

**Evergreen**

---

### Stage 2: Views but No Visits (not clicking through)
**Definition:** A "view" = your listing appears on a search results page. A "visit" = someone actually clicks on your listing.
**Problem:** People see your listing in search but don't click.
**Cause:** Almost always an image problem.

Three specific issues:
1. Your main mockup image
2. The thumbnail vibe
3. The clarity and quality of the design

> "The problem for not converting a view into a visit is almost always going to be your main mockup image, the thumbnail vibe, or the clarity and quality of the design. So it's basically all the image related issue."

**Evergreen**

---

### Stage 3: Visits but No Interactions (no favorites, no add-to-cart)
**Definition:** An "interaction" = customer favorites the listing, adds to cart, or shares it.
**Problem:** People visit your listing page but don't engage.
**Cause:** One of three issues:

1. The design is bad (low quality)
2. You don't have good info cards
3. The pricing is too aggressive

> "If you're getting views and visits on your products, but they're not interacting by favoriting or adding that product to the cart, it's likely due to one of these three issues."

**Evergreen**

---

### Stage 4: Interactions but No Conversions (not buying)
**Problem:** People favorite or cart your listing but don't purchase.
**Cause:** This is described as "super uncommon" — once you get interactions, sales usually follow. But if not, check:

1. Product variations — colors and sizes you're offering (colorways)
2. Trust signals:
   - Do you have bad reviews?
   - Have you tried uploading a video to those listings?
   - Does your shop overall look trustworthy?
   - Is there anything "weird" about the page turning them away?

**Evergreen**

## Scaling Winners

> "Any product that has sold is a winning product."

### The Core Rule
> "Don't waste time guessing what's going to work when the market tells you something. Listen and then scale immediately."

### Step-by-Step: How to Double Down on a Winning Listing

1. **Duplicate the listing** — start with what's working
2. **Modify the duplicate slightly** — change one or more of:
   - Main listing image
   - Colorway
   - Add a video
   - Change the description
   - Slight modification to the design itself
3. Keep the core elements the same — do not reinvent the wheel
4. Publish the new listing as a separate listing (not a variant) to multiply visibility

> "Keep the core elements the same. Don't reinvent the wheel, just build on what's proven to work."

### The Multiplier Formula
> "As soon as one design and one listing sells, especially early on in your journey, you can create five more variations of that same exact design using different mockups and colorways as your main listing image, and this gives you six listings in total, all targeting the same validated niche with unique presentation each time."

**Why Etsy rewards this:**
> "When a listing from your shop sells, Etsy's algorithm has validated that that type of product works well in your shop. They're going to continue testing that product as well as other products in the search algorithm, and they're going to push them harder than they would have before."

### Cross-Niche Translation (Advanced Scaling)
Once a design style or template works in one niche, translate it directly into a different niche:
- Example: A nurse profession design → duplicate the template → swap profession to accounting
- > "This is the best way to scale your shop because it's the most organic, it's the most natural. And you're just building on the success that you're generating for your own shop."

**Evergreen**

## Templates & Systems

### Listing Volume Targets
- **Sweet spot:** ~20 new listings per day, ~5 days per week
  - Enough volume to grow quickly without spamming
  - Manageable to systematize
  - Leaves room for quality improvement
- **Aggressive sprint example:** Up to 50 listings/day, 1,700 listings in a single month (described as "a little overkill")
- **Part-time minimum:** Even 5–10 quality listings per week can still work long-term
- **Part-time rule:** > "Consistency beats intensity"

> "After you've gotten to 20 listings per day, you can start to improve the quality more than you improve the quantity that you're posting."

**Evergreen**

### Part-Time Seller System
For sellers with only a few hours per day:
1. **Pre-batched design days** — dedicate full days to one task (e.g., all design creation) instead of doing all steps daily
2. **Reusable title and tag templates** — know your SEO framework before you sit down to work
3. **Strict folder organization** — everything in the right place so you're not scrambling
4. **Time blocking** — assign specific time slots to specific tasks (research / designing / uploading)

> "Full time sellers can basically brute force their way through any inefficiencies or any mistakes that they make. But part time sellers, you really can't waste a minute... Your constraint is time. So optimization becomes your superpower."

**Evergreen**

### The Live Workflow (from 45-minute recorded demo)
Order of operations in a single working session:
1. Research niches using Listing View (sort by monthly sales/revenue) — ~15 minutes
2. Write down sub-niche ideas as you scroll (don't validate yet, just collect)
3. Validate one saying: search it on Etsy, confirm multiple large shops (10k+ reviews) sell the same saying → indicates no single copyright holder
4. Open design hacking board in Kittl, duplicate the project, rename it for this niche
5. Create design variations using 4–5 different font/layout combinations (~9 minutes for 7 designs)
6. Export at 300 DPI, black and white versions separately
7. Upload white versions to Printify/Printify template via Prelist; add variant for dark colors (white + ivory shirts)
8. Build title keyword string: open 3–5 top-selling competitor listings, copy/paste their title keywords into a text editor separated by commas, paste into Prelist
9. Run Photoshop mockup automation script while Printify uploads process in background
10. Publish listings from Printify
11. Add tags: open Listing View optimizer, grab tags from proven listings, update all listings
12. Upload ~6 mockups per listing via Listing View, add info cards to slots 7–8, delete printer default images from slots 9–12
13. Publish all updates

Total time for 7 listings: ~45 minutes

**Evergreen** — tools (Prelist, Kittl, Listing View) are **Check** for current availability/pricing.

### Design Hacking Board Reuse
- Name the board after the niche (e.g., "sarcasm design hacking")
- When you have a proven layout/template, duplicate the board and fill in the new saying — you skip the layout-creation step entirely
- > "If you have really high quality layouts and templates... we're just going to fill it in with the new idea."

**Evergreen**

## Key Takeaways (3–5 bullets)

- **The core loop is non-negotiable.** Every day follows: customer service → research → design → publish → customer service → fulfill. This compound routine is what builds revenue, even when it doesn't feel like it.

- **The diagnostic funnel gives you a specific fix for every stage.** No views = fix SEO. Views but no visits = fix your main image. Visits but no interactions = fix design quality, info cards, or price. Interactions but no sales = fix trust signals and product variants.

- **Any sale is a signal to multiply immediately.** When one listing sells, create 5 more variations using different mockups and colorways before doing anything else. Etsy's algorithm actively rewards shops that convert, and variations give you multiple shots at the same validated concept with minimal extra work.

- **20 listings/day at quality beats 50 listings/day at speed.** After reaching 20/day, improve quality rather than increasing quantity. Part-time sellers should batch tasks by day and rely on strict systems — optimization is the only lever they have.

- **Free Etsy automations are underused.** Daily sales (scheduled in advance) + all three email automations (abandoned cart, favorite, thank you) cost nothing and compound over time — the instructor's example shows $8,000 in added profit from favorite-item automation alone on a moderately successful shop.
