# Gap Analysis — Course vs Our Workflow
Source: Alek's course insights (01–05) vs WORKFLOW.md

---

## Missing Completely (in course, not in our workflow at all)

**1. The Sub-Niche Method — pre-design slogan/saying validation**
The course's central strategy: search a broad category on Etsy, find a specific saying repeated across multiple listings from different large shops, search that exact saying to confirm low-competition + proven demand, validate it is copyright-safe by confirming 3,000+ review shops all use it. Our workflow has no equivalent step. We go from "niche" straight into design brief and creation. This is described as "the real sauce" and "the section that will make more sales than the rest of the course combined."
Source: 02-design-system.md

**2. Permanent 50% off sale (doubled list price strategy)**
Almost all successful Etsy POD shops run a permanent 50% off sale at all times. To do this, the list price must be set at 2× your actual target sell price, and the sale brings it to the correct price. Our WORKFLOW.md has a different approach: list at ~33% above sell price, run 20% off for 60 days. We have a sale strategy, but it is a temporary launch sale — not a permanent one. This is a structural difference in how the Etsy listing is built.
Source: 01-niche-research.md

**3. Listing volume as the primary growth lever**
The course is explicit: the #1 driver of revenue is the number of quality listings. Alek's benchmark: ~1,000 quality listings = ~$1,000–$2,000/month profit; he has made $1/listing/month on average over 6 years. Target is 20 listings/day. Our workflow is designed to produce one polished listing per cycle and has no volume-building system, daily cadence, or batch target.
Source: 04-marketing.md, 05-scaling.md

**4. Daily core loop / operational routine**
The course defines a daily repeating routine: (1) customer service first, (2) research, (3) batch design, (4) upload/publish, (5) customer service again, (6) fulfill orders. Customer service is deliberately done before fulfillment to catch cancellation requests. Our workflow has no daily operational cadence — it is a one-time launch process with monthly performance tracking.
Source: 05-scaling.md

**5. Etsy email automations (abandoned cart, favorite, thank you)**
Three Etsy-native automations that run permanently once configured: ~45–50% off for abandoned cart, ~55% off for favorited items, ~55–60% off for thank-you (repeat purchase). The coupon percentages are set to match the daily sale so customers cannot stack discounts. Alek calls these "pretty underutilized." Our workflow has no mention of these at all.
Source: 05-scaling.md

**6. Daily sales scheduling**
A 50% off sale must be manually created each day (Etsy does not allow automation of this). The workaround is to schedule sales one to four weeks in advance. This is distinct from email automations above. Our workflow does not address this.
Source: 05-scaling.md

**7. Mockup research process (outlier score validation)**
The course has a specific process for selecting mockups: search Etsy for your product, use the outlier score to find listings performing above average, identify which mockups those outlier listings use, then buy those specific mockups from Etsy sellers. The principle: pick mockups proven to convert, not mockups you personally like. Our workflow (Phase 6) says to add "5–10 listing photos" and lists the types (flat lay, model, close-up, size chart) but has no research-driven process for which mockups to select.
Source: 03-listing-seo.md

**8. Mockup automation (Figma component/variant system or Photoshop script)**
The course teaches batch mockup generation: either via Figma's component system (free, fastest) or a Photoshop script that auto-generates all design × mockup combinations. The output is all mockup variants generated in one run, then the best combination is selected per listing. Our workflow has no mockup automation — it just says to add photos to the listing in Etsy.
Source: 03-listing-seo.md

**9. Design hacking system (structured element extraction)**
The course describes a specific design research method: extract fonts, color palettes, graphic styles, effects, and slogans from best-selling listings as separate elements, then reassemble them into original designs. This is distinct from simply looking at competitors. Our workflow does a competitor analysis (price, keywords, colors from description text) but has no structured design element extraction process.
Source: 02-design-system.md

**10. Black + white design versions per listing**
Every design must be produced in two color versions: black design on white/ivory shirts only, and a slightly off-white (#FBB1B or similar — not pure white) design on all other/darker shirts. This serves both visual quality and print longevity (double ink layer). Our workflow mentions "transparent background PNG" and 4500×5400px resolution but does not address the two-version requirement.
Source: 02-design-system.md

**11. Info card requirements (color chart + sizing guide as standard listing images)**
The course treats two info cards as required for every apparel listing: a color chart showing all offered colors with names, and a sizing guide with measurements. These must be placed in consistent numbered image slots across all listings so bulk-updating them later takes seconds. Our WORKFLOW.md Phase 6 lists "size chart" as one of four suggested photo types — it is treated as optional context rather than a required asset in a fixed slot.
Source: 03-listing-seo.md

**12. Listing video (doubles conversion rate per Etsy's own stat)**
The course states Etsy's own data shows listings with videos convert at 2× the rate of listings without, and only ~28% of listings use videos. Our workflow has no mention of listing videos.
Source: 03-listing-seo.md

**13. Scaling winners: 5-variation multiplier**
When a listing sells, immediately create 5 more variations of that same design using different mockups and colorways as the main listing image. This gives 6 listings targeting the same validated concept. Our workflow has no "scaling winners" step — performance tracking exists (Phase 6.4) but there is no defined action when a listing converts.
Source: 05-scaling.md

**14. Diagnostic funnel (Views → Visits → Interactions → Conversions)**
The course provides a specific four-stage diagnostic: no views = fix SEO; views but no clicks = fix main image; clicks but no interactions = fix design quality, info cards, or price; interactions but no purchases = fix trust signals and variant selection. Our workflow tracks "views, visits, conversions, top search terms, revenue" monthly but has no diagnostic framework for what to do when numbers are bad.
Source: 05-scaling.md

**15. Seasonal listing calendar (4-week lead time rule)**
Indexing takes 2–3 weeks; fulfillment adds another week. Therefore seasonal listings must be live at least 4 weeks before the target date. Our workflow has no seasonal planning component.
Source: 04-marketing.md

**16. Max 12 non-required variants / buyer fatigue rule**
For color choices (non-required variations), cap at 12. Offering more causes buyer fatigue and kills conversions. Use Printify's best-selling variants data to select the top 6, then add 6 more from competitor or personal preference. Our workflow selects top 12 colors × 8 sizes via market-insights.md — the 12-color limit is aligned, but the selection methodology (Printify variants data vs. competitor text scraping) differs.
Source: 01-niche-research.md

**17. Customer service system (replacement listing + canned replies)**
The course describes a specific replacement listing priced at exact print cost + Etsy listing fee, used when a customer orders the wrong size or color. The customer buys the replacement; the seller keeps all original profit. Also: pre-written quick replies for all common support scenarios. Our workflow has no customer service system.
Source: 05-scaling.md

**18. Shop sections (organized by high-level niche, set up before bulk publishing)**
Etsy allows up to 12 shop sections. The course recommends creating them before bulk publishing so listings can be assigned in bulk. Our workflow has no mention of shop sections.
Source: 03-listing-seo.md

**19. Loss leader variant strategy**
Set one unpopular variant (e.g., small white) to a lower price so the listing displays cheaper in search results. When the buyer selects their real size/color, the price adjusts upward. Our workflow has no loss leader tactic.
Source: 01-niche-research.md

**20. Product template (build once in Printify, copy for all future listings)**
Build the first product in Printify with a placeholder design, set all variants and pricing, save it, and reuse it as the template for every future listing. Our workflow creates products individually through the API without an explicit template-reuse system.
Source: 01-niche-research.md

## Quick Wins for Save-the-Date T-shirts (apply before publishing)

**1. Validate your slogan against the sub-niche safety check**
Before publishing, search the exact phrase "save the date shirt" (or the specific wording on your design) on Etsy. Confirm that multiple large shops with 3,000+ reviews are all selling shirts with that same saying styled differently. If they are, you have both demand confirmation and a copyright safety signal. If the exact phrasing is unique to one shop, reconsider or modify the wording.
Source: 02-design-system.md

**2. Fix the pricing structure before publishing — decide: temporary sale or permanent 50% off**
Right now our workflow has a 20%-off 60-day launch sale baked in. The course says almost all successful shops run a permanent 50% off sale. If you want to match the market standard, you need to set the Printify list price at 2× your actual target sell price BEFORE publishing. Once listings are live and shared, changing the base price disrupts the sale math. This decision must be made now.
Source: 01-niche-research.md

**3. Create two design versions: black on white/ivory only, off-white on everything else**
If your current design is a single color, you need a second version. Export: (a) black design for white and ivory shirts only; (b) a slightly off-white version (not pure white) for all other colorways. Applying the wrong version to dark shirts will produce a muddy print. This must be done before uploading variants to Printify.
Source: 02-design-system.md

**4. Add a color chart and sizing guide as fixed image slots (slot 7 and 8)**
These are listed in the course as required for every apparel listing. A color chart showing all offered colors with names reduces buyer confusion and customer service questions. A sizing guide with measurements reduces returns. If you create them now and place them in consistent image slots, you can bulk-update them later across all listings in seconds.
Source: 03-listing-seo.md

**5. Research which mockups outlier listings are actually using — don't pick by eye**
Before selecting your listing photos, search "save the date shirt" on Etsy, identify the listings with the best performance signals, and note which mockup styles they use (model lifestyle shots, flat lays, etc.). Buy those specific mockups rather than using stock options. This is a one-time research step that directly affects click-through rate.
Source: 03-listing-seo.md

**6. Add adjacent keywords to your tags and title**
"Save the date shirt" targets one audience. Adjacent keywords that a buyer could still convert on: "engagement party shirt," "bride squad shirt," "wedding party shirt," "future bride shirt," "bridal party tee," "getting married shirt." These should be in the remaining tag slots. One of these adjacent terms may outperform the primary phrase.
Source: 04-marketing.md

**7. Set up all three Etsy email automations before you get your first sale**
These are configured once and run permanently: abandoned cart offer (~45–50% off), favorite item offer (~55% off), thank you offer (~55–60% off). They must be set up before the first sale to capture every interaction from the start. The coupon percentage should match or slightly exceed your daily sale to prevent stacking.
Source: 05-scaling.md

**8. Schedule your first daily sale in advance**
Etsy does not allow automating the daily sale — it must be set manually. Schedule a 50% off sale (or whatever sale percentage you choose) out at least one week in advance now, before the listing goes live, so it is running from day one.
Source: 05-scaling.md
