# Section 8 — Mockups (We Scale / Facebook Ads)
Source: We Scale (Meg, Eric + narrator), lines 701–791

⚠️ **Context:** This mockup strategy is built for **Facebook paid ads → Shopify store**, not Etsy organic. The flatlay-only rule and testing framework are specific to that channel. Principles around quality and realism are universal.

---

## Why Mockups Matter

**Evergreen**

"Mockups are like movie posters. A bad poster — just title on a colored background — even great movies, nobody goes. Why? It doesn't communicate anything and it looks low-budget. But a good poster shows the vibe, the tone, and the quality. And immediately you know this is for me."

Three components of every mockup:
1. **The shirt** — must be compelling, realistic, with proper lighting and shadowing
2. **The background** — clean, on-brand, not distracting
3. **The props** — niche-relevant, 2–3 items max

"A mockup is the background, the shirt, and any objects around it — not the design itself."

---

## Good vs Bad Mockups

**Evergreen**

### Bad mockup failures
- Shirt appears to be floating (no shadowing, no grounding)
- Design is off-center or too small — unreadable on mobile
- Too many props — design can't be seen clearly
- Shirt takes up too little of the frame
- Fake-looking (no wrinkles, flat lighting, pasted-on design feel)
- Non-relevant background for the niche

### Good mockup criteria
- **Shirt takes up majority of the image** — sleeves nearly touching the corners
- **Realistic shadowing** — shirt looks like it's actually resting on a surface, light shining down
- **Nothing on top of the design area** — props stay below the print zone
- **2–3 props max**, niche-relevant
- **Clean, simple background**
- Natural wrinkles in fabric, design looks printed (not pasted)

"Good: clean background, realistic, design clearly visible. Bad: cluttered scene, fake-looking, design not readable."

## Flatlay Only (for Facebook Ads)

**Channel-specific** — applies to Facebook paid ads, not necessarily Etsy organic

Wscale tested lifestyle mockups (models wearing shirts) vs. flatlay across 600+ community members and concluded flatlay wins consistently for Facebook ads.

**Why flatlay works for ads:**
- "We want people to picture themselves in the shirt, not see someone else wearing it"
- Clean presentation style performs better in Facebook feed
- Easier to generate at scale with AI tools

**What to avoid (for Facebook ads):**
- Lifestyle mockups with models
- Hanger shots
- Elaborate staging
- Obvious stock photos

## Specs

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Image size | 1080 × 1080px |
| Ratio | 1:1 (square) |
| Perspective | Top-down flat lay |
| Style | Professional, realistic |

## How to Generate Mockups (AI Method)

**Their tool** — uses proprietary "Mockup Gem" GPT + Gemini image generation

Their workflow:
1. Open their "Mockup Gem" GPT → type "[niche] mockup" → it outputs structured image prompts
2. Copy prompts into Gemini (Nana Banana Pro) with image generation enabled
3. Review outputs — reject anything with: floating shirt, cluttered frame, off-brand props, shirt too small
4. Pull accepted mockups into Canva to crop/resize so shirt fills the frame fully
5. Target: 10 usable mockup variations

**Alternative without their tool:** Write your own Gemini prompts describing:
- Niche and props (e.g. "engagement niche — wedding rings, champagne flutes, flowers")
- Top-down flatlay perspective
- Clean neutral background
- Shirt with natural wrinkles and realistic shadows
- Shirt taking up majority of 1080×1080 frame
- Nothing covering the chest/print area

Iterate prompts — Gemini doesn't always get it right. Remove specific phrases (e.g. "knotted at the bottom") to adjust shirt presentation.

## The 10-Mockup Testing Framework

**Facebook ads specific**

Make 10 variations before testing. Test one variable at a time:

**Phase 1 — Test the mockup**
Run all 10 mockups with the same design to find which frame/setting converts best. The winner becomes the template for all 100 products.

**Phase 2 — Test the designs**
Lock in the winning mockup. Now run different designs within that mockup to find which design resonates most.

**Phase 3 — Store optimization**
Once a winning design + mockup combo is identified, optimize the store and scale.

Goal: isolate variables so you know *why* something is working before scaling spend.
