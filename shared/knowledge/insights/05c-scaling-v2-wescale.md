## Scaling Your Catalog (4.7)

### Core philosophy: catalog growth is a volume + iteration loop

**Evergreen** — "It really only takes one design to completely change the game." The entire catalog-scaling system is built around finding that one thread and pulling on it.

**Evergreen** — "The winners compound because one winner turns into three to five variations." Each winning design should spawn 3–5 blue-ocean iterations (not minor tweaks), and each of those can produce further winners.

**Evergreen** — Never make minor tweaks to a winner ("removing the sun," "changing the font"). These address the same micro-audience. Always iterate at the concept level — different jokes, different styles — to open a new conversation with a new segment of the same niche.

**Evergreen** — When iterating, first isolate (1) the concept, then (2) the style/color. The concept is the primary driver; the graphic is secondary.

**Evergreen** — The 50/50 rule: "Half of our designs can come from iterations off of the ones that got less than a dollar cost per click. Half of the new designs, so 50% from iterations, 50% from net new research." Research never stops.

**Evergreen** — "The top 1% of print on demand sellers are the ones who are making seven designs per day." The stated weekly goal is 25 new designs. Volume is the fundamental input.

**Evergreen** — Winning design criteria (catalog ad context): 3–5 link clicks at under $1 cost per click. Even one qualifying design out of 100 is considered a strong result.

**Evergreen** — "Volume is the best way to learn." Stated win/loss ratio: "one to 20 or 30, and that's after doing this for years."

**Evergreen** — The iteration workflow: (1) Find the winner by cheapest cost per click. (2) Analyze: what is the concept? What is the style? (3) Iterate: change the concept, keep the structure. (4) Use AI (ChatGPT + Gemini in parallel) to brainstorm concept branches, then generate design prompts from those.

**Evergreen** — AI design workflow specifics: run the same prompt in ChatGPT and Gemini simultaneously to maximize output per hour. Limit image-gen prompts to 5–10 at a time (more causes quality degradation). Upload screenshots of top-performing designs and instruct the model to analyze art style and colors — not concepts — for style guidance.

**Evergreen** — Loser analysis is equally valuable. Designs that get no engagement signal: wrong genre (e.g. "peaceful/eco-friendly" vs. "humor/sarcasm"), too many colors, or vague concept. These inform what not to pursue.

**Check** (Facebook Ads-specific) — The weekly testing cycle described (ads Thursday–Sunday, design Monday–Wednesday, analyze Monday) is tied directly to Facebook catalog ad mechanics. The underlying principle — weekly test/analyze/iterate rhythm — is platform-agnostic, but the specific day structure is DTC/Facebook-only.

**DTC-only** — "Catalog ad is auto-updating, meaning anything new that we add to the store automatically shows up in that ad without us having to do anything." This refers to Facebook Dynamic Product Ads. Etsy has no equivalent.

**DTC-only** — The cost-per-click metric (target: under $1.00) is a Facebook Ads metric. Etsy equivalent would be click-through rate or conversion rate from search impressions.

**Evergreen** — Designs that consistently sell in the humor/sarcasm/relatable-joke genre outperform peaceful, eco-friendly, or artistic designs in POD. "It's not a coincidence that the majority of the best sellers are humor-based."

**Evergreen** — "Design volume equals sales volume. At the very least, it equals the sales ceiling." Studied across members doing 10k+ and 50k+ in sales; design volume is the #1 input correlated with success.

**Evergreen** — Time-blocking recommendation: 30 min Monday to analyze results, 1 hour Monday/Tuesday/Wednesday to make designs, time Wednesday night or Thursday morning to upload. Phone on do not disturb. Treat it like a doctor's appointment.

## Turn Traffic Into Sales (4.8)

### Funnel structure and order of operations

**Evergreen** — "The biggest mistake people make here is they try to do all of them at the same time." Only work one funnel variable at a time: first traffic quality (cost per click), then conversion rate, then average order value.

**Evergreen** — "You can't force results. But consistent inputs, consistent inputs over time create the foundation for success." Focus on inputs (daily actions), not outputs (sales/ROAS).

**Evergreen** — The three-metric funnel: (1) Traffic quality. (2) Conversion rate (CR). (3) Average Order Value (AOV). ROAS/revenue is the output of these three — "ROAS is not an actual metric. It's just an equation."

**Evergreen** — Conversion rate is broken into three sub-metrics: add-to-cart rate → reach checkout rate → sessions converted. Diagnose and fix each in sequence from top of funnel downward.

**Evergreen** — Target benchmarks:
- Add-to-cart rate: **6–8%**
- Reach checkout rate: **5–6%**
- Sessions converted (overall conversion rate): **2.5–3%** (achievable higher — narrator cites 3.78% average, with days at 4.7–5.5%)
- AOV initial baseline: ~$30–35 (one shirt). Target: above $45 before considering scaling.

**Evergreen** — Diagnostic rule: "If your conversion rate is low, the first step — you don't do anything else — you look at add-to-cart and if add-to-cart is less than 6% you focus on the collection page and the product page." Only two pages influence add-to-cart rate; only the cart drawer influences reach-checkout rate; only the checkout page influences sessions-converted rate. Do not make changes to other pages.

**Evergreen** — "It is a million golden BBs. It is a lot of little teeny tiny tweaks that ultimately add up to a high converting store." Conversion rate optimization is not a silver bullet.

**Evergreen** — Mobile-first imperative: "80-plus percent of our traffic is going to come from mobile. So start with this." All store optimization should be evaluated on mobile first.

**Evergreen** — "Above the fold" principle: every critical element (product images, add-to-cart, color swatches, price) must be visible without scrolling on a phone screen. A logo that is too large, a size chart on its own line, or long product titles all push content below the fold and hurt add-to-cart rate.

**Evergreen** — Treat POD stores as an "impulse store," not a "planned store." "They meet you where you are. They just simply lay out the products you're looking for, few others that are related, and they shorten the aisle as much as possible." Minimal distractions, no widgets that pull attention away from the buy path.

**Evergreen** — Collection page: drive all traffic here (not the product page). Reason: customers arrive in a "scroll mindset" from social. A collection page continues that passive browsing experience. "Collection page converts better" — tested repeatedly.

**Evergreen** — Product page priorities: color swatches close to top, add-to-cart button in a distinct color (black, red, or green — not used elsewhere on the site), button text in ALL CAPS, trust micro-copy directly below the button (e.g. "30-day money-back guarantee," "50,000+ sold").

**Evergreen** — Cart drawer / checkout: keep it extremely clean. Free shipping threshold bar is the primary conversion lever here. "We don't have a ton of recommended products. We don't have like a order guarantee, anything like that. We just have the bare minimum." Do not distract from the checkout button.

**Evergreen** — Product titles on collection cards: two to three words max plus "t-shirt." Long titles push price to a second line and hurt conversions.

**Evergreen** — AOV improvement — the two levers: (1) Set a free shipping threshold that requires adding ~3 products (e.g. $75+). (2) Fill the catalog with designs that are thematically related to the best-seller so buyers have obvious companion purchases. "It is substantially easier to get someone to buy more than it is to get somebody to buy at all."

**Evergreen** — Printify default shipping settings actively hurt AOV. They charge a reasonable rate for one item but escalate sharply for two or three. Override these manually.

**Evergreen** — "The fastest way to optimize your conversion rate is to simply look at an example of a top performing store." Reference stores named: House of Chenasos, Life is Good, Into the AM, Busted Tees.

**Evergreen** — Green light criteria before scaling spend: (1) Cost per click consistently under $1. (2) Conversion rate above 2.5%. (3) AOV above ~$45. When all three are met, ROAS above 2.0x follows automatically from the math.

**Evergreen** — "A product that is incredible. It speaks to them on a personal level. It is funny. It is sentimental. It expresses the ethos of your niche, of your brand, like your tribe of people... I've seen conversion rates double overnight with one new design like that added." Product quality/relevance is the ultimate lever for conversion — it overrides all store optimization.

**DTC-only** — The specific funnel flow (Facebook ad → collection page → product page → cart → Shopify checkout) is Shopify-specific. On Etsy, the funnel is search → listing page → Etsy checkout. Etsy does not have a cart drawer or a collection page in this sense.

**DTC-only** — Recommendation to use three-step checkout, specific Shopify payment settings, and PayPal integration ("about 40 to 50% of our sales come through that") are Shopify-specific.

**DTC-only** — The W Scale Calculator and W Scale GPT mentioned as diagnostic tools are proprietary to their program.

## Profit is a Choice (5.4)

### The P&L discipline

**Evergreen** — "Profit is a choice. Profitability in your business is a decision that we have to make and commit ourselves to as the business owners." Framed as a mindset commitment, not an automatic byproduct of revenue.

**Evergreen** — "The truth is profitability is a math equation. It's all that it is. Dollars in, dollars out. We know how much we should be spending based on the benchmarks. And then anything that is outside the benchmarks, we then work to bring that back in."

**Evergreen** — P&L is non-negotiable: "If you're going to continue with this business from this point forward, it is a non-negotiable that you run this as frequently as possible, at bare minimum a weekly basis, ideally daily." Stated result: "just from the things we're about to cover, you can be with no additional effort in the business — this stuff takes about 15 minutes each week — means four to five times more profit."

**Evergreen** — "What gets measured gets grown. Run it daily so we're able to track revenue, control costs, and optimize profits."

**Evergreen** — "Sales are vanity, profit is sanity, and cash is reality."

**Evergreen** — Personal case study: improved margins from "roughly 3 to 5% at low volume to upwards of 20% plus" purely by running a P&L and controlling costs. The 0-to-7-figure brand ran $430k in revenue with $60–70k net profit (~14–16% margin) in one month.

**Evergreen** — The P&L formula: Income − Expenses = Net Profit. EBITDA referenced as the formal equivalent.

**Evergreen** — Cost benchmarks (their DTC Shopify brand — use as reference, not gospel for Etsy):
| Line item | Target % of revenue |
|---|---|
| Cost of goods (Printify) | 40–42% |
| Ad spend | 25–33% (implies ~3x ROAS target) |
| Transaction fees | ~4–5% (US-only) |
| Overhead (software, apps) | ~3% |
| Refund rate | ~1% |

**Check** — These benchmarks assume Facebook Ads spend as a major cost line. On Etsy, there is no equivalent mandatory ad spend line; "cost of goods" and Etsy fees/ads would need to be substituted.

**Evergreen** — Overhead creep is a silent killer: "I had at one point we stopped tracking this and it was up to about 10% of revenue. It got insane. We were paying like $35,000 per month in just software fees, which we did not need. It should have been like five grand." Audit every subscription; list them monthly and track as a % of revenue.

**Evergreen** — Two additional profit levers specific to their scale:
1. Printify volume rebate: "our brand has done enough that we are making about 5% back on cost of goods" — tiered rebate program exists inside Printify.
2. Cash-back credit card: "Capital One 2% cash back that we put all transactions on" — added $4,600 in one month on their volume.

**Evergreen** — Accrual accounting for daily P&L: spread monthly fixed costs (software, etc.) across each day (e.g. $3,000/month = $100/day entry) so no single day looks artificially unprofitable when a bill hits.

**Evergreen** — "Positive ROAS but cash flow is a struggle" is a common trap. Revenue and profitability are not the same thing. Members were hitting this exact wall before running a P&L.

**DTC-only** — Specific mechanics of pulling numbers (Shopify analytics, Printify invoices by country, Triple Whale, Facebook ad account spend) are Shopify/Facebook-specific. The principle of pulling each cost source weekly is universal.

**DTC-only** — Recommendation to separate international vs. US Printify invoices, and not to target international until 10k+ in monthly sales, is relevant context but DTC-specific in execution.

## Key Takeaways (3–5 bullets)

1. **Design volume is the master input.** Every metric — cost per click, conversion rate, AOV, ROAS — can be partially overridden by a single great design. "One winning design can double conversion rate overnight." On Etsy, this maps directly: listings volume + iteration on bestsellers is the core lever.

2. **Work one funnel variable at a time, in order.** Traffic quality first, then conversion rate, then average order value. Never optimize AOV before you have consistent conversions. Never optimize the store before traffic quality is proven. The order of operations prevents wasted effort and isolates causation.

3. **Iterate at the concept level, not the execution level.** Minor tweaks (font, color, leaf count) address the same micro-audience. Concept-level iterations (different joke, different emotional angle, different graphic metaphor) open new audiences while carrying forward proven appeal signals.

4. **Run a P&L weekly at minimum.** "Four to five times more profit" was achievable with zero additional revenue — purely from knowing the numbers and cutting overhead creep. Track cost of goods as a % of revenue every week. Overhead drifting above 3% of revenue is a red flag.

5. **Consistency over intensity.** "25 new designs every single week" beats a one-time burst of 100. The weekly rhythm — test, analyze, iterate, repeat — compounds. "That is the action that took Sloth from this to this." Sustainable input rate trumps occasional intensity.
