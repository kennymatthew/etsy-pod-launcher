## 3 Criteria of a Winning Product

**Criterion 1: Evergreen + High Sales Volume**
- "I only sell long term best selling products like t shirts, mugs and posters because they've been proven to work for years and they all have a high sales volume." — **Evergreen**
- Competition = demand signal: "It might seem scary to a lot of people because there's a ton of other sellers also selling this exact same product, but that's the exact reason why I was successful, because there was a ton of demand for it." — **Evergreen**
- Avoid low-volume evergreen products: the weekender bag example — evergreen but not enough sales volume to build a sustainable shop around. — **Evergreen**
- Key rule: "Picking something that's evergreen means that it'll sell today and far into the future, and high sales volume means there's a lot of competition and a lot of demand for it." — **Evergreen**

**Criterion 2: Good Profit Margin**
- "I always aim to make at least a $7 profit per sale, which is usually around a 30% profit margin, plus or minus maybe 5%." — **Check** (specific to current Printify/Etsy fee structure)
- Sweatshirt example: costs ~$20.28 (product + shipping), priced $30–$35, leaves ~$7–$11 profit after Etsy fees. — **Check**
- "$7 of profit per sale and more gives us enough margin so that we can scale with some light paid ads on top of all the organic traffic." — **Evergreen** (principle)
- Scale math example: "If we make a $7 profit per sale and we have a catalog of, let's say, 300 products, and each one of those listings makes three sales per month. That's going to get us to $6,300 of profit per month." — **Evergreen** (illustrative)
- ⚠️ The 30% figure is calculated after Etsy fees — not a simple markup. Use the Listing View calculator to verify for your specific product and price point.

**Criterion 3: Adaptable / Versatile**
- "Versatility is key, and that's because I need to be able to sell a product to almost anyone." — **Evergreen**
- A versatile product should support designs for: professions (nurses, teachers, doctors), interests (coffee, reading, travel), hobbies (gaming, crafting), and viral/trending niches. — **Evergreen**
- Negative example: "If you're selling something like a shower curtain, it's going to be really hard to make trending designs for that product." — **Evergreen**
- Apparel-specific tip: "I would want to sell an apparel item because they're very versatile in terms of designs. They're clearly bestsellers and they're evergreen. Since people are always needing to buy new clothes." — **Evergreen**

**Recommended starting product (at time of recording):**
- Comfort Colors 1717 unisex garment-dyed t-shirt — ranked #2 on Printify best-seller list for the year. — **Check** (rankings change; verify current list)
- Printify best-seller page shows weekly, monthly, and yearly rankings by print sales volume. — **Check**

## Building a Competitor List

**Goal:** Find at least 5 shops successfully selling the same product you want to sell.

- "We're not here to reinvent the wheel, and so the quickest path to true success is to follow what's already worked for other successful sellers." — **Evergreen**
- "We're not here just to copy someone" — the list is for reference: keywords, niches, pricing, mockups, and problem-solving. — **Evergreen**
- Criteria for adding a shop: "If I had to spend an entire year to grow a business and I ended up with their shop and their level of success, would I be happy with it? If the answer is yes, then I would add that shop to my competitor list." — **Evergreen**

**Method 1 — Listing View Shops Database:**
1. Open Listing View → Shops Database tab.
2. Filter by shops opened in the last 180 days (6 months) — or up to 12 months.
3. Sort by total sales (since all shops are relatively new, total sales = proxy for performance).
4. Add columns for primary category and secondary category.
5. Scan for shops with matching product category (e.g., "t-shirts"), click to favorite/add to watch list.
6. Updated feature: use the category filter (type "shirts") + max age filter (12 months) to narrow results directly. — **Check** (Listing View UI may change)
7. Database had "over 900,000 shops at time of recording." — **Check**

**Method 2 — Listing View Chrome Extension on Etsy:**
1. Install the Listing View browser extension; pin it.
2. Search Etsy for the specific product + brand (e.g., "Comfort Colors graphic tee").
3. Scroll results; look for listings with "outlier" badge or strong monthly sales badge.
4. Click a listing → use the extension dropdown to see shop-level stats (monthly sales, monthly revenue, total sales).
5. Favorite the shop directly from the extension dropdown.
- Reason for this method: "It allows us to find shops selling the specific variation, like the Comfort Colors t shirt. Since there's a ton of different brands of t shirt, we want to make sure that the specific variation is selling on Etsy." — **Evergreen** (principle)

**Pro tip — listing-to-sales ratio:**
- "Shops with tons of sales that have fewer listings typically mean that their average listing quality and conversion rate is a bit better." Prefer these. — **Evergreen**
- ⚠️ Exception: shops selling copyrighted/Disney designs can spike and then get shut down by Etsy — don't include these. — **Check**

**What the competitor list helps you learn:**
- Which keywords drive traffic and sales
- Which niches to target
- What markups/prices convert
- Which mockups to use
- Which specific product variations to offer

## Variants & First Product Setup

**Two types of variations:**
1. **Required variations** — customer has no real choice (e.g., size: they order their own size). Offer all available options.
2. **Non-required variations** — customer chooses based on preference (e.g., color). Cap at a maximum of 12 options.

- "For any non required variations…I would recommend offering a maximum of 12 variation options to that customer." — **Evergreen**
- Rationale: "If you offer more than 12, the customers can often get something called buyer fatigue where they're not going to be able to make a decision…and they'll never end up buying." — **Evergreen**
- "A very common mistake for beginners is offering way too many options in their listing, thinking that it's going to get them more sales when in reality it just causes buyer fatigue." — **Evergreen**

**How to pick which color variants to offer:**
- Option A: reference competitor listings — look at which colors they offer (their dropdown, not just the info card).
- Option B (preferred): use the Printify Insights Dashboard best-selling variants table:
  1. Filter by product type (e.g., "unisex garment dyed t-shirt") and model (e.g., "Comfort Colors 1717").
  2. Table shows top 20 best-selling color variants out of the full catalog (59 colors for Comfort Colors 1717).
  3. "A super simple rule of thumb: maybe you choose to sell the top six variations based on this data, and then you come through and choose six more based on whatever you personally want to sell." — **Check** (Printify dashboard UI)

**Etsy variation rules:**
- "An Etsy listing can have up to and a maximum of two variations." — **Check** (platform rule, may change)
- Each variation combination (e.g., small + black) gets its own SKU, price, and quantity — all pre-filled by Printify automatically.
- Enable "show all variations as available for purchase" toggle in Printify so that if one warehouse is out of stock, another warehouse fulfills the order. — **Check**

**Product template approach:**
- Build the first product in Printify with a placeholder text design, set all variants and pricing, save it — then reuse it as a template for all future listings. — **Evergreen** (workflow principle)

**Info cards (required before launch):**
- Required minimum info cards for an apparel listing: (1) sizing guide, (2) color chart.
- Sources: Printify's default preloaded images (basic/sterile look); buy pre-made on Etsy (~$4–$17); or create from scratch in Kittl.
- Alek's approach: bought a mockup pack + sizing guide for ~$17 total, then assembled a custom color chart in Kittl (2000×2000 px artboard).
- Use black text on light shirts, white text on dark shirts for the color chart labels. — **Evergreen**
- ⚠️ Info cards should match shop branding; Printify's defaults look "a little sterile and a little bit print if I coded" — his words.

## Pricing & Shipping Strategy

**Base pricing rule:**
- Target: at least $7 profit per sale, ~30% profit margin (±5%). — **Check** (fee-dependent)
- Comfort Colors 1717 example verified in Listing View calculator: sell at $17.99, charge $5.99 shipping. Production cost $10.72 + shipping cost $3.99 = $14.71. After Etsy fees: 27% profit margin, $6.50/sale — "within the 5% threshold." — **Check**

**50% off sale strategy (Etsy norm):**
- "Almost all successful shops are going to be running a sale at all times."
- Run a permanent 50% off sale. To do this, double your actual target price and set that as the list price.
- "I typically like to run a 50% off sale on all of my products, since it makes the math really easy, because it just means that I need to double the price of all of my products so that when I run that 50% off sale, it comes down to the correct price that I actually want to sell the products at." — **Check** (Etsy platform behavior; strategy may shift)
- Example: list at $35.98 → 50% off sale brings it to ~$18 → add $5.99–$6 shipping → total revenue ~$24. — **Check**
- ⚠️ Printify's "estimated profit" in the quick overview should show ~50% higher than target, because the sale halves it to the correct price.

**Loss leader strategy (optional):**
- Pick an unpopular variation (e.g., small white t-shirt), lower its price by a few dollars so the listing shows up cheaper in search results. When the customer selects their real variant, the price increases.
- "I personally wouldn't recommend doing such an aggressive loss leader, where it jumps from $7 all the way up to 19. By doing it by a few dollars can definitely help you stay competitive." — **Evergreen** (principle)
- Example: drop small white variant from $26 to $19 (−$7). — **Check** (specific dollar amounts are illustrative)

**Larger size pricing:**
- Small–XL: lower base cost (e.g., $12.41 for Comfort Colors 1717).
- 2XL–4XL: higher cost due to more material and less popular = warehouse space premium.
- Set 2XL–4XL at the same profit margin percentage (not the same flat price) to stay in profit on those SKUs. — **Check**

**Shipping setup:**
- Charge shipping separately rather than baking it into product price. Reason: "charging shipping is a great way to add a little bit more revenue and incentivize larger orders."
- Domestic (US): charge slightly more than Printify's default (e.g., $5.99 first item, $1.99 each additional). — **Check**
- International: increase further to pad for lost-parcel risk (example cited: Canadian Post strike).
- Add an "everywhere else" catch-all location with a fixed rate (e.g., ~$12 first item, $5 additional, based on Printify's ~$10/$4 cost). — **Check**

**Etsy Free Shipping Guarantee ($35 threshold):**
- "Any order that is $35 or more [subtotal, excluding tax and shipping] is going to qualify for free shipping."
- Etsy grants priority placement in search results to shops that offer this guarantee.
- "It will make it so that any customer who places an order over $35, which will mean they'll need to order at least two items from us, will qualify for free shipping." — **Check** (Etsy policy; threshold or benefit may change)
- Alek's recommendation: enable it. "I don't think it's necessarily required, but…I don't see why you wouldn't do it."
- ⚠️ The $35 threshold means a single ~$18 item does NOT qualify — the buyer needs to purchase 2+ items. This naturally incentivizes multi-item orders.

**Shipping profile — one-time setup:**
- Create a single reusable shipping profile in Etsy Shop Manager → Settings → Shipping Settings.
- Name it "standard shipping."
- In Printify publishing preferences, select this profile so every new product published automatically uses it.
- "If we ever need to update it, it updates across all of our listings simultaneously." — **Evergreen** (principle)

## Key Takeaways (3–5 bullets)

- **Competition confirms demand.** High competition on t-shirts, mugs, and posters is not a reason to avoid them — it is evidence that buyers exist in large numbers. Use Printify's best-seller rankings to validate before building. (**Evergreen**)
- **Max 12 non-required variants.** Offering more than 12 color options causes buyer fatigue and kills conversions. Use Printify's best-selling variants data to pick the top 6, then layer in 6 more from competitors or personal preference. (**Evergreen**)
- **Double your price; run a permanent 50% off sale.** This is the standard Etsy POD operating model. Your Printify list price should be ~2× your actual target sell price, so the sale brings it to the right number. Verify margins in Listing View calculator before committing. (**Check**)
- **Build one product template first.** Set all variants, pricing, and the shipping profile once in Printify, publish it, and reuse it. Every future listing is a copy of this template — not a rebuild from scratch. (**Evergreen**)
- **Competitor list = your cheat code.** At least 5 shops. Use it to reverse-engineer keywords, niches, pricing, mockups, and which specific color variants are actually converting — before you design anything. (**Evergreen**)
