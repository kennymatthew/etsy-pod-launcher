## Product Setup Process

- The workflow is: complete all 100 designs first, then upload to Printify, then publish to Shopify. Design phase is treated as entirely separate from upload phase.
- Meg works in **batches**, not one-by-one: upload all designs to Printify in one session, then handle all Shopify edits in one session. She considers this significantly faster than going product-by-product.
- **Speed hack — duplicate template:** After uploading the first product, use Printify's "duplicate" button to copy all settings (price, colors, shipping profile, tags). Then only swap the design file and spot-check settings. Saves significant time on repeat products.
- Printify settings to configure before any uploads: store name, business info/address, ship-from address (recommends Printify's default, not personal address), order rerouting limit (~$2, exact product matches only), order submission time (she sets 11:55 p.m. daily), and Printify Choice enabled.
- **Order rerouting:** Set max additional cost to ~$2 to stay within expected margin. "If I was waiting until like the max additional cost was $100, like that could be really expensive for me as the business owner."
- **Order submission timing:** Setting a specific nightly submission time (she uses 11:55 p.m.) makes cost-of-goods tracking cleaner — all charges group to end of day for easy reconciliation.
- For mockup selection: "We'll just pick this one design because we'll end up switching all of them out anyways." — minimal mockup work inside Printify; mockup overhaul happens later on Shopify side.
- Product naming in Printify: she deletes all pre-filled description/tag fields and adds only one tag ("t-shirt"). Title is just the design name (e.g., "Bad Lie"). All detailed editing happens via Shopify's bulk editor.
- Set "show all variants as available for purchase" even if out of stock (avoids false out-of-stock display on storefront before settings are fully configured).
- After publishing, Shopify requires a one-time manual step per product to connect color meta fields (t-shirts category > variants > color). No bulk method exists for this yet (as of filming). This makes swatch colors display correctly on the product page.

**DTC-only:** All Shopify-specific steps (bulk editor, category meta fields, BMP product tagging, product visibility) apply only to Shopify stores.

## Variant & Color Selection

- **Target: 6–8 colors per product.** Not more, not fewer.
  - Tested with 14 colors: conversion rate dropped to 1.5% ("in the dumps"). "People got overloaded with decisions."
  - "We've tried it with 14. We've tried it with three. Six to eight is a really good range."
  - Target conversion rate: "anywhere around 3 to 4%."
- **Core "traditional" colors always included:** black, white, navy. "These are going to be the most popular colors that you sell." Navy historically outperformed on past brands.
- **Add 3–4 more colors that match niche/branding** (e.g., feminine niche can include pink; golf brand used Kelly green).
- **Do not upload a color if the design doesn't pop against it.** "If it doesn't look good on Printify, it's not going to look good." Rule: if design is dark, skip dark shirt colors (black, charcoal, military green) unless design is specifically light-colored.
- **Design-per-color trick:** Printify allows assigning a different design file to a specific color variant (e.g., white design on dark shirts, black design on light shirts). This lets one product work across both light and dark colorways.
- **Sizes offered: Small through 3XL** (she excludes 4XL and 5XL by default, though notes they can be added).
- Global fulfillment toggle: keep off when targeting US only — turning it on restricts available color options.
- Nothing is permanent: "All of these can change later. We can always add more colors if we want. We can remove colors if we want."

**Evergreen** (color logic and decision paralysis principle apply on any platform).

## Pricing Logic

- **Tested price range: $14.99 to $34.99** for t-shirts. "We've played around with the pricing a lot."
- **Current pricing strategy (Gildan 64000, Printify):**
  - S–XL: **$29.99**
  - 2XL: **$30.99**
  - 3XL: **$31.99**
  - 4XL / 5XL: continue adding ~$1 per size
- **Rationale for $29.99:** "We're not competing with our competition on a marketplace next to anybody." On Etsy competitors sell at $17.99–$19.99; DTC branded store can command a premium because the customer isn't comparison-shopping side-by-side.
- **Cost breakdown at $29.99 (approximate):**
  - Production cost (shirt + printing, 1 location): ~$7.91
  - Shipping: ~$4.75
  - Shopify fees: ~2.9% + $0.30 ≈ $0.90
  - **Profit before ads: ~$16–$20**
  - **Profit after ad costs: ~$8–$12 per shirt**
- **Single print location only.** Two print locations add ~$4 to production cost, requiring higher retail price. "Right off the bat, we don't need to be doing any of that."
- **Shipping: standard shipping only.** ~5 days to customer, ~$4.75 cost. Economy saves ~$1 but risks more "where's my order" inquiries. Expedited not recommended at launch.
- **Starting COGs at 45–50% even with Printify Premium.** "Which is definitely on the higher side, but Printify Premium helps to bring that percentage down." Printify Premium (~$29/month after free trial) provides up to 20% off product costs.
- Avoid Printify's suggested price of $10.99: "You wouldn't even make any money off of that."

**Evergreen** (cost logic and margin math apply regardless of sales channel; specific platform fees are DTC-only).

## Launch Sequencing

- **Upload all 100 designs before going live / running ads.** The full catalog is needed before marketing begins.
- **Recommended upload schedule (80/20 action plan):**
  - Day 1: upload 20 products
  - Day 2: upload 20 products
  - Days 3–5: upload remaining 20 per day
  - "By the weekend, all of your designs are now uploaded into Shopify and then we're just making some bulk edits on Shopify and essentially your store is ready to run ads."
- **Speed benchmark:** ~1 minute per design once familiar with the process. "If it's taking you about five minutes for five designs, you should be done in about 20 minutes."
- No staggered launch of select "winner" products first — the model is to launch all 100, then let ads identify winners.
- Products are set to **active/visible immediately on publish** from Printify. No manual review gate before going live.
- Before uploading any new Printify products, she tags and sets any existing BMP (automation) placeholder products to draft in Shopify, to avoid confusion between BMP products and manually uploaded products.

**DTC-only** (upload schedule and Shopify sequencing logic are platform-specific; the principle of batching and launching full catalog is evergreen).

## Key Takeaways (3–5 bullets)

- **6–8 colors, not 14.** Too many color options kills conversion. Start with black, white, navy, then add 3–4 niche-relevant colors. Fewer choices = higher checkout rate. Tested and confirmed.
- **Price at $29.99 for S–XL, add $1 per extended size.** This delivers ~$16–$20 gross profit per shirt before ads on the Gildan 64000. Don't undercut yourself trying to compete on price — branded DTC removes the side-by-side comparison problem entirely.
- **Use the duplicate template method in Printify.** Create your first product perfectly, then duplicate it for every subsequent upload. Only change the design file and spot-check colors. This compresses a multi-hour task to roughly 1 minute per product at pace.
- **Upload all 100 products before running ads.** The model relies on volume to surface winners; launching with 5–10 products is not the strategy here.
- **Single print location only at launch.** A second print location adds ~$4 to production cost with no meaningful benefit for a new store. Add front+back designs only after the brand is established.
